🚀 New paper out in Environment and Behavior! 🌍

🥕 In this preregistered study, we shed new light on the cognitive mechanisms underlying pro-environmental behavior by examining how individuals whose daily practices contribute to climate change mitigation (i.e. vegetarians) make decisions with real-world environmental consequences.

🖥️ A total of 172 vegetarians and 196 non-vegetarians completed a variant of the Carbon Emission Task, repeatedly choosing between two options that varied in monetary rewards and CO2 emissions. Beyond comparing their final decisions, we used MouselabWEB process-tracing technology to examine how vegetarians and non-vegetarians searched for and prioritized information about personal gains and environmental costs.

📈 Our findings show that vegetarians were more likely to select the pro-environmental option than non-vegetarians. Crucially, this difference was especially pronounced when decisions involved high personal costs, low environmental benefits, or uncertainty.

👀 Process-tracing data further revealed that vegetarians allocated earlier and greater attention to environmental information, and that this attentional focus significantly predicted pro-environmental choices.

🌱 Together, these results suggest that vegetarians may rely on a more value-driven decision-making style that goes beyond cost–benefit trade-offs and places greater weight on environmental considerations. In this sense, vegetarianism may serve as a behavioral proxy for environmental commitment.
